# Neon Sign Sites

We rebuild weak websites of US local service businesses (lawn care, mobile detailing, ...) and offer the new site by email, with a free preview. Owner: Alon (sole proprietor in Israel, עוסק פטור). Store Passport (repo `kelon33/site-translator`) is a separate business: don't touch it from here.

## Offer (keep the site, terms, emails and previews consistent with it)
- **Own it:** $299 one-time. Hosting, SSL and 1 small edit a year included for the first year, then $49/year (about $4/month). More changes $25 each, or $19/month care. Not renewed: offline after a 30-day grace period, and we send them the site files. Always mention the $49/year next to "no monthly bill"-style claims.
- **Monthly:** first month free, then $49/month. Card at signup, first charge on day 31. Minimum 2 paid months after the free month ($98), then cancel anytime; cancelling during the free month costs nothing. One free month per business. The monthly card is the highlighted one on the site. Say "minimum 2 paid months" (never just "2-month minimum") so nobody reads it as 2 months in total. Up to 2 small edits a month (text, photos, prices, hours). Switch to owning for $299 anytime.
- **Small edit (defined in the terms and FAQ, 2026-11-27):** one change to what is already on the site: text, prices, hours, phone, service areas, or swapping or adding up to 5 photos; done within 2 business days. New pages, sections, features or a redesign are quoted separately (from $25). Unused edits don't roll over.

- **Emails carry no price (Alon, 2026-11-28):** the first email and the follow-up say who we are, link the preview and ask "Want it?"; the price is given after they say yes (and is on the preview's bar and closing block). `EMAIL_VERSION` in `pipeline/email.mjs` marks the wording: the subject A/B test counts only sends of the current version, so bump it when the body changes.

## Repo and publishing
- `site/` is neonsignsites.com (home, terms, privacy, 404). `generator/` builds a customer site from `demos/<slug>/site.json`, with one kit per trade (`kits/detailing.mjs` dark/glossy, `kits/lawn.mjs` bright/green, `kits/painting.mjs` navy/gold on the lawn layout, `kits/holiday.mjs` Christmas lights: a real photo of lit snowy trees, berry red and gold). `pipeline/` goes from a lead to a preview. `leads/` holds lead research.
- **Every push to `main` goes live in about a minute.** Cloudflare Workers Builds runs `node deploy.mjs --dry`, then `npx wrangler deploy` (`wrangler.jsonc`). Previews are at `neonsignsites.com/p/<slug>/`, never indexed (robots.txt, X-Robots-Tag, meta tag).
- `npm test` must pass before pushing.
- The Cloudflare token on the environment (proxy-injected for api.cloudflare.com) has Pages Edit, Account Settings Read and DNS Edit (neonsignsites.com zone). It has **no Workers permissions**: Worker settings and custom domains are done by Alon in the dashboard. Wrangler uploads don't work from a cloud session (the proxy replaces wrangler's upload token), so use git pushes.

## Pipeline
- `npm run find -- --trade lawn,detailing --city "Austin, TX"` finds new leads with Google Places (Text Search only, a minimal field mask, next page only while it pays off), checks their sites and emails for free, and writes `leads/found-<date>.json`. `--dry` shows the plan and worst-case cost first. Every request is counted in `leads/places-usage.json` (commit it) against `config.json` `places.monthlyRequestCap` (3,600 ≈ $126 at list price); a search already run is skipped unless `--again`. Needs `GOOGLE_PLACES_API_KEY` (or `NEON_GOOGLE_PLACES_API_KEY`) in the environment. The search and the site check can run apart: `--no-check` in the `neon-sign-sites` environment (has the key; its network must allow `places.googleapis.com`), then `npm run find -- --check leads/found-<date>.json` in `neon-sign-sites builder` (open network). If nearly every site fails to load, the check stops instead of marking them broken. Each trade searches several phrases (`TRADES` in `pipeline/find.mjs`; `--phrases 1` for the main one only, worst case 3 requests per phrase and city). Sites come out as `weak site` (main pitch), `decent site` (works but has clear gaps, listed in `improvements`: a separate, second pitch) or `strong site` (not pitched); `--check <file> --again` re-checks a file for free. Every save sorts the file weak → decent → strong (then broken, not checked) and writes `leads/found-<date>.md`, a readable copy with one section per category and a count per trade (commit it with the .json).
- `npm run find -- --trade lawn,detailing --next 3` takes the next unsearched cities from `leads/cities.json` (ordered so consecutive cities are in different regions). Test trades (`TRADES` entries with `test: true`: pressure, pool, windows, and small one-person trades such as petwaste, sprinkler, stump, sod, lights, boat, rv, dent, windshield, handyman, painting, junk, grooming) are search-only, to measure their weak-site rate; `npm run batch` never builds them until the trade is promoted (drop `test`). For one-person trades use `--min-reviews 5` and smaller towns. Promoted trades that borrow a kit (`dent` and `boat` → detailing; `painting` and `lights` have their own kits on the lawn layout) get their own default wording from `TRADE_WORDS` in `pipeline/copy.mjs`, so no lawn or detailing leftovers show; add an entry there when promoting another one.
- **Where and what to search (Alon, 2026-11-28):** mid-size towns and suburbs (about 30k-300k people) before big cities (`leads/cities.json` lists them first); follow the seasons: Christmas lights until mid-December, pressure washing, pool and lawn from February and March.
- `npm run batch -- --file leads/found-<date>.json --count 5 [--dry]` builds previews for the best leads (email, kit, not built, not on do-not-contact; weak sites first, then decent, trades in turn; within each, highest `leadScore()` first: 15-150 reviews, rating 4.5+, an owner's or named address, photos on their site) with `npm run lead -- --place <id>`, and writes `leads/batch-<date>.md` to review them by. Review every preview as usual.
- `npm run queue -- --plan` writes `outbox/<date>.md`: today's emails in full, for Alon's approval, within each mailbox's daily limit (`config.json` `sending.mailboxes`: no `start` date means it sends nothing). It never sends; it also writes `outbox/<date>.json`, the exact texts that get sent. `npm run send -- --approve [--skip 4,7]` records Alon's approval of the list; `npm run send` (run it in the background: the six mailboxes send in parallel, each spacing its emails 2 to 5 minutes apart, and each email waits for its business's 8:00 local time) sends them through Gmail, each from its planned mailbox (`NEON_GMAIL_CLIENT_ID`/`_SECRET` and `NEON_GMAIL_TOKEN_<NAME>_<TLD>` per mailbox), checking every rule again before each one, and logs each in `leads/sent.json` (commit it). `--dry` shows what would go. `npm run queue -- --record <slug> --from <mailbox>` logs an email sent by hand. `npm run replies` checks the six mailboxes (inbox and spam) for replies to our emails (same thread, the address or company domain we wrote to, or quoting our preview link or subject; warm-up mail is ignored), bounces, and the site's "Make it live" / preview-request emails (kind `site-form`), into `leads/replies.json`; a clear "no" goes on `do-not-contact.json` at once. `npm run answer -- --reply <id> --file <answer.txt> [--dry]` sends Alon's approved answer from the mailbox that got the reply, in the same thread (never without his approval of the exact text). It refuses neonsignsites.com mailboxes, businesses already emailed (beyond their one follow-up), do-not-contact entries, and planning while there is no postal address. **One follow-up** (Alon, 2026-11-28): 4 days after the first email, only if there was no reaction at all (reply, no, bounce, site form), from the same mailbox in the same thread (`followupBody()` in `pipeline/email.mjs`); planned first each day, counted in the mailbox's daily limit, and shown in the list Alon approves. Never a second follow-up. **Address check:** `npm run mx` (builder environment, needs DNS) records in `leads/mx.json` whether each address's domain accepts mail; planning leaves out domains with no mail server, so they never bounce.
- **What gets replies** (`npm run stats`, `pipeline/stats.mjs`, also on the dashboard): reply rate (replies, site forms, payments; not "no" or bounces) by trade, state, site kind, Google rating and review count. Once a group has 30 first emails (`MIN_SENT`), the daily search and the builder favour the best groups (trades and states first) and drop clearly weak ones.
- **Reply playbook** (`docs/reply-playbook.md`): ready answers for common replies (price, is this a scam, how did you get my info, yes, changes, already have a web guy, not now, domain and email, examples, no, angry). The reply check gives it to the model that drafts the suggested answer; add an entry when a new kind of reply shows up twice.
- **Uptime** (`npm run uptime`, run by the 2-hourly reply check): HEAD checks of the site, payment pages, recent previews and every customer site (plus `launch --check`); only problems are reported to Alon.
- **Email wording (agreed with Alon 2026-11-27):** `compose()` in `pipeline/email.mjs`: their personal opening line, "So I built you a new one, with your own ..." and the link, "If you like it, it can be live this week. First month free, then $49/month, or $299 to own it.", then "Want me to put it live?" (photo pitch: asks for 5 or 6 job photos, "Want me to finish it?"), then "If you'd rather talk it through, call me at ..." and the signature. The full terms are on the site, before anyone pays. Planning rewords older drafts into this wording. **Subject A/B test:** the plan alternates A `<Name> – new website (preview)` and B `Quick question about <their-site.com>`; `npm run replies` prints the reply rate of each. After about 100 emails, keep the winner (tell Alon).
- `verify()` also drops trust claims their site does not make (licensed, insured, guarantee, family-owned, owner does the work, no subcontractors, same-day, 24/7...) and notes the AI wrote to itself ("the site says..."). Decent-site emails name the gap the site check found. When a preview ends up with no gallery (fewer than 4 usable photos of their work), it is still built and sent, with the photo pitch: `site.json` `pitch: "photos"`, and the email says their site shows almost none of their work and asks for 5 or 6 job photos to finish it. `npm run batch -- --place <id>,<id>` builds exactly those leads.
- **Wording library:** `npm run wording` saves the general wording of every preview in `demos/` (headline, section titles, steps; name and town as placeholders; nothing with a number) to `wording/<trade>.json`. New previews start from it (the AI improves on it; `--no-ai` uses it as is, free). Services, prices, reviews and facts always come from the business's own site, never from the library; a reused line with a trust claim is kept only if their site makes it too. Run it after each reviewed batch and commit `wording/`.
- **Checks without AI** (`pipeline/checks.mjs`, run by `npm run lead`): reviews are kept only when written into their own pages (in the HTML their server sends); any sign of Google's (a review widget such as Trustindex, Elfsight or WP Review Slider, "Posted on Google" or "Google review" labels, a Google stars badge) drops them all, along with lines that quote them ("customers say ..."). A detailer found by a boat search whose site is about cars is built as car detailing. A steps title counts its steps; towns don't go under "We also do". The "Why ..." heading uses the whole wordmark. The AI also checks the logo taken from their site is theirs (not a Google badge, a client's or their web designer's); if not, the header shows the name only. A detailer from a boat search that does cars too gets all its work shown, not only boats.
- **Library first:** once a trade's wording library has `config.json` `wording.libraryFirstMin` (8) reviewed previews, its headline, titles and steps are used as they are, and the AI is asked only for the business's facts and photos (intro, services, points, reasons, hours, reviews, photo choice). `--model <id>` overrides `aiModel` for one lead (to compare models).
- `npm run lead -- --lead "<name>"` reads their site, picks photos and logo, writes the wording, renders the site and writes `preview.html`, `desktop.jpg`, `phone.jpg`, `email.txt` and `check.txt` in `demos/<slug>/`. `--no-ai` uses the free kit wording; `--fresh` ignores an existing hand-written `site.json` (otherwise its wording is kept).
- **AI model and cost (Alon, 2026-11-27):** stays Sonnet 5.5. Haiku 5.5 was tested on 5 leads: about 14x cheaper, but it picked stock photos and dropped real ones on 4 of 5. Approved: run the AI step through the Message Batches API (half price), so 180 previews a day fits Alon's $100 a month of free API credits. Plan in `docs/handoff/builder.md`; not built yet.
- The AI wording needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `NEON_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (an environment variable; the cloud environment has the NEON_ one). The model is `config.json` `aiModel` (Sonnet 5.5, a few cents a lead; Haiku 5.5 is the cheap option). `verify()` drops reviews not on their site word for word and any number not in their site text or Google facts.
- Always look at `desktop.jpg`/`phone.jpg` and `check.txt` before showing a preview to Alon.
- **No photo of their own:** when a preview has no usable hero photo, the page uses a licensed real photo for its trade (`generator/stock.mjs`, sources and licences in `generator/assets/CREDITS.md`), credited in the hero and noted in `check.txt` as not their work. Add a photo there for each new trade (CC0, CC BY or attribution-only; check the licence on the file's own page). `noStockHero: true` in a site.json turns it off.
- **Rule: soft colours and soft transitions.** No plain white pages: each kit uses a soft tinted background that matches its palette (lawn sage, painting stone, holiday cream, detailing deep slate), with cards a shade lighter. Wherever two section colours meet (hero, bands, tinted and dark sections, footer) the change is a gradient, never a hard edge. A new kit follows the same rule (see the RULE blocks in `kits/lawn.mjs` and `kits/detailing.mjs`).
- **Rule: the first screen reads as a whole page.** The headline, every hero button and the Google rating must be fully visible without scrolling on a laptop (1366×768), a desktop (1440×900) and phones (390×844, 375×667). `npm run fold` checks every preview (and `npm run lead` writes any problem into `check.txt`); a new kit must pass it before its first preview is shown.
- `demos/ericks-auto-detailing` and `demos/lawn-sculptures` are hand-written: don't regenerate their wording.

## Launching a customer site
- `npm run launch -- --slug <demo> --domain <their-domain.com>` renders the live version (no preview bar, indexable) into `customers/<domain>/`, registers `www.<domain>` with Cloudflare for SaaS (custom hostnames: 100 free, then $0.10/month each) and writes `connect-email.txt`. `--check` tells whether it's live. A push to `main` publishes it: `deploy.mjs` copies it to `dist/c/<domain>/` and `worker.js` serves it on their domain by Host header.
- The customer adds one CNAME (`www` → `config.json` `customerTarget`) and forwards `<domain>` to `www.<domain>` at their registrar (no apex on Cloudflare's non-Enterprise plans). Their other records, and so their email, are never touched. They choose: do it themselves from the email, or let us do it (delegate access at their registrar, or a screen-share). Never ask for their password. One-click Domain Connect comes later.
- Set up and tested end to end on 2026-11-27 (custom hostnames, fallback origin `sites.neonsignsites.com`, Worker route `*/*`). The "neon customer domains" token (SSL and Certificates Edit on neonsignsites.com, DNS Edit on neonsignsites.net) is proxy-injected for api.cloudflare.com in the main environment; scripts send a placeholder bearer.
